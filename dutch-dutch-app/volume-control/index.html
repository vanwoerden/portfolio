Dutch & Dutch control app / Volume control

Volume control

The problem

Dutch & Dutch speakers don't ship with a physical volume control. Some users rely on external devices to control the volume: a streaming device, an external gain controller, a mixing desk, etc.

Still, adjusting the volume is the most frequent action when playing or working on music. And this is why the volume control is front and center in the app.

The existing volume control had a few discoverability and usability issues that needed fixing:

How do I change the volume?

Press and drag within the circle to adjust the volume. Not easily discovered! And your fingers cover the dB value while adjusting it.

How do I mute the speakers?

No visible mute button. Tap the circle to mute and un-mute the speakers.

How do I set a sleep timer?

Press and hold to put the speakers in sleep mode or set a sleep timer. Simple, right?

How do I cancel the sleep timer?

Press and hold again to stop the sleep timer.

The verdict

None of these actions are obvious. There are no visual cues that tell users how to complete any of them. And because the Dutch and Dutch home audience skews a bit older, there's a strong need for the app's interface to feel as familiar and obvious as possible.

The circle with zero affordances was leading to support tickets.

The solution

A breakdown of the changes I made:

  • A volume wheel makes it extra clear this is a control that can be moved up and down.
  • An accent line quickly indicates the current relative volume level
  • Subtle haptic feedback for each 0.5 dB increment
  • dB level above the wheel is visible while adjusting
  • Extra large dB level while sliding the wheel provides extra precision and control
  • Plus, minus, and mute buttons to match most users' expectations (think of typical remote controls)
  • Sleep becomes a card in the "room view", with the options showing on tap
  • Lots of room for other room settings to be displayed

What's with the wheel?

Why not a typical volume slider? It’s very easy to move a simple volume slider from 0 100. That was easy.

Too easy, in fact.

Dutch & Dutch speakers go loud. Not just wake up the neighbors loud; ear-damaging loud, if turned up too quickly while sitting too close. We needed some inertia built into the volume control.

A synthesizer's pitch wheel has obvious use cues and some friction built in. Mimicking a control from the real world that had proven itself over decades felt like a natural fit.

The other details like the indicator line, the haptic feedback, and the larger than life dB level while dragging were somewhat inspired by !Boring's World's Most Satisfying Checkbox; the volume control is the most used control in the app. It should be extremely obvious. But not boring!
